A few habits go\ a long way. 1. Give context, not just a task Tell the tool who you are, what you're working on, and what good looks like. “Review this\ NDA from the perspective of the receiving party” beats “review this NDA.” 2. Ask for the reasoning Request exact quotes and citations. If an answer can't point to a source, treat it as a\ draft to verify rather than a conclusion to rely on. 3. Work in small steps Break a big task into pieces, summarize, then flag risks, then propose redlines. Each step\ is easier to check than one giant prompt. 4. Reuse what works When you land on an instruction that works, save it. A skill library means you never paste\ the same instructions twice. 5. Keep a human in the loop The goal isn't to remove the lawyer, it's to remove the rote work so the lawyer can focus\ on strategy. Always review before anything goes out the door.
